Methodology & sources

Data sources

  • BLS QCEW (Quarterly Census of Employment & Wages) — annual-average employment, establishments and wages by county, pulled from the QCEW Open Data Access service (data.bls.gov/cew/data/api/{year}/a/area/{fips}.csv; free, no key). County totals use the row where industry_code=10 (all industries) and own_code=0 (all ownerships). The refresh auto-detects the latest published annual year.
  • BLS LAUS (Local Area Unemployment Statistics) — the latest monthly unemployment rate per county, from the BLS public timeseries API (series LAUCN{fips}0000000003). A BLS_API_KEY is used when present but is not required.
  • Census ACS 5-year (optional) — median household income and population, pulled only when a CENSUS_API_KEY is configured; otherwise those columns are omitted and everything else still renders.
  • BEA Regional (optional) — per-capita personal income (table CAINC1), pulled only when a BEA_API_KEY is configured; adds a PCI column to the county table.

Market-vitality score

Each county is percentile-ranked (0–100) across the 33 footprint counties on four metrics, and the ranks are combined into a weighted average. Percentile-ranking makes the metrics comparable regardless of units and keeps the score robust to outliers. The default weights are:

  • Employment level — 30% (higher is better): the size of the local labour market.
  • Average weekly wage — 25% (higher is better): income level and lending capacity.
  • Low unemployment — 25% (lower rate scores higher): labour-market tightness / health.
  • Establishment density — 20% (higher is better): establishments per 1,000 workers — the breadth of the business base and the pool of potential commercial borrowers.

The weights are fully adjustable on the Vitality Ranking tab; the blend renormalises over whichever components are available for a county, so a missing unemployment reading never blanks the score. Establishment density is deliberately a per-worker ratio rather than a raw count, so it measures business breadth independently of sheer size (already captured by the employment component).

Limitations

  • QCEW annual averages lag the present by roughly 6–9 months; the unemployment rate is a single recent month and can be noisy.
  • Figures are county totals across all industries — no industry or firm-size breakdown, and QCEW covers only UI-covered employment (most, not all, jobs).
  • The vitality score is a relative ranking within this footprint, not an absolute or national benchmark; re-weighting changes the ranking by design.
  • Counties are queried by the FIPS codes in the shared footprint list and labelled by the authoritative county for that FIPS, so the data and label always agree.
  • ACS median household income / population are 5-year pooled estimates (ACS 2022), not single-year figures.

Refresh: python economic/refresh.py — auto-detects the latest QCEW year. Scheduled weekly via GitHub Actions.

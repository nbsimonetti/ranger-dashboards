Residential mortgage terms

Owner-occupied and 1–4 unit lending: conventional purchase and refinance, government (FHA/VA/USDA), jumbo, home equity and home improvement. One row per market × product. Rates are note rates actually originated; LTV, DTI, term and total loan costs are medians for the same loans. Structure columns give the share carrying an adjustable intro period, interest-only payments, a balloon or a prepayment penalty. Small samples are flagged — treat those as directional.

Competitor profiles

Every lender originating in West Texas, with volume, pricing posture and where they operate. Rate rank compares a lender's median rate against the market median for the same products — cheaper lenders are the ones taking your deals on price.

Branch presence & balance-sheet posture

Banks with branches in the footprint (FDIC Summary of Deposits), alongside their call-report earning-asset yield and cost of funds. A low cost of funds is a structural pricing advantage — those competitors can undercut and still make margin.

Commercial real estate & business terms

Income-producing and business-purpose credit: multifamily (5+ units) and business-purpose dwelling-secured loans from HMDA, plus SBA 7(a) and 504 pricing. These are commercial exposures underwritten on the property or the business, not on an owner-occupant — which is why they are separated from the residential book.

SBA business & 504 pricing

HMDA only reaches dwelling-secured lending, so conventional C&I and non-residential CRE are not in it. SBA disclosures carry real initial note rates, terms and sizes by market and lender — the best public read on business-loan pricing.

What is missing from the commercial view

Conventional (non-SBA) commercial mortgages and C&I loans are not publicly disclosed at loan level anywhere. Neither are DSCR requirements, covenants, guaranty structure or relationship pricing. The Competitor Profiles tab carries each bank's call-report earning-asset yield and cost of funds, which is the closest public proxy for how a competitor can price commercial credit — use it directionally, not as a quote.

Methodology, sources & what this can and cannot tell you

What you are looking at

Loan pricing and structure reconstructed from public loan-level disclosures — not advertised rate sheets. Every rate here is a note rate on a loan that actually closed, so it reflects real executed pricing including borrower-specific adjustments.

Sources

  • HMDA loan-level (CFPB data browser) — the core. Post-2018 HMDA reports note rate, LTV, DTI, term, total loan costs, discount points, and flags for interest-only, balloon, prepayment penalty and introductory (ARM) periods, per loan, per lender, per county. Filtered to originations.
  • SBA 7(a) & 504 FOIA — initial note rate, fixed/variable, term and size for business lending, by lender and county.
  • FDIC call reports — each bank's earning-asset yield (INTINCY) and cost of funding (INTEXPY), as a pricing-capacity proxy for products the loan-level sources do not cover.
  • FDIC Summary of Deposits — branch presence by county, so competitor rankings reflect who is physically in the market.

How confident to be in each number

OBSERVED — note rate, loan amount, LTV, DTI, loan term, total loan costs, discount points, ARM/IO/balloon/prepayment flags, lender identity and county. These are reported facts from HMDA and SBA filings, not estimates.
INFERRED — the product labels (built from HMDA's loan type, purpose, lien status, unit count, business-purpose flag and the conforming-limit flag); ARM share proxied by a non-zero introductory rate period; and the CRE/C&I pricing proxies derived from call-report yields, which are portfolio-wide averages rather than new-money quotes.
NOT AVAILABLE without an application — CRE debt-service coverage requirements, covenants, guaranty structure, deposit-relationship pricing concessions, negotiated exceptions and internal credit overlays. None of this is public. Where you see no data for a product, that is why — the dashboard does not fill the gap with guesses.

Limitations worth stating out loud

  • HMDA lags about a year. The year in the header is the most recent published. In a moving rate environment, treat levels as relative-to-market, not as today's absolute quote — the spread between competitors ages far better than the level does.
  • HMDA is dwelling-secured only. Commercial real estate and C&I are thin here; the multifamily and business-purpose rows capture only what happens to be dwelling-secured. SBA and the call-report yields carry the rest.
  • Small samples are unstable. Rural West Texas counties can produce a "median" from a handful of loans. Sample size is shown on every row and small ones are flagged; the deal tool refuses to give a verdict below a usable sample.
  • Rate spread is not the note rate. This dashboard uses the reported note rate directly and does not substitute HMDA's rate-spread field.
  • Credit unions are not in FDIC data. They appear in HMDA if they lend, but their branch presence and balance-sheet columns will be blank.

        Cost of Funds (FDIC) = FDIC's published INTEXPY field — interest expense to earning assets, already annualized by FDIC. This is the industry-standard COF measure every banker recognizes; typical TX community bank reads 1.0–2.5%.
        COF on IB Deposits = our own calculation, (annualized EINTEXP) ÷ Interest-Bearing Deposits × 100. Scoring & Methodology

        
This platform uses two opposite scoring lenses. They answer inverse questions and are colored inversely so they can never be confused.


        
        

          

            
🟢 Market Entry Opportunity (offensive)

            
Higher = better market to ENTER. Scores TX metros. A high score means an under-served market with below-median rates and room for a competitive new entrant — where Ranger should grow. Green = pursue.

          

          

            
🔴 Competitor Watch (defensive)

            
Higher = bigger threat to DEFEND against. Scores individual banks. The "Rate Aggressiveness" component ranks on interest-bearing cost of funds — the rate a bank pays to win rate-sensitive money — because that, not the blended total, is what determines who out-bids us for the marginal depositor. Red = a threat, not a model to copy.

          

        


        
        

          
Two cost-of-funds measures — when to use which

          

            Total COF (blended) = FDIC INTEXPY, interest expense ÷ earning assets. The bank's all-in funding cost. A low Total COF usually signals a strong non-interest-bearing (NIB) relationship book — cheap, sticky deposits that don't move on rate. Use it to gauge a bank's structural funding advantage.


            IB COF (marginal rate) = annualized interest expense ÷ interest-bearing deposits. The rate a bank pays only on rate-sensitive money. This is the number that matters when competing on rate. NIB deposits are relationship-driven and won't follow a rate offer, so the marginal depositor is captured at the IB rate — and a bank with a high IB COF will out-bid you for that depositor even if its Total COF looks low. The Competitor Aggressiveness score ranks on IB COF for exactly this reason, and the Market Entry tab's "Marginal Rate to Win" column shows the median IB COF of banks in each market — the rate floor a new entrant must beat.
          

        

        

          The green-light overlap rule. The two lenses inform each other. A market scores high on Entry Opportunity partly because it lacks aggressive competitors — so the cleanest expansion targets are markets with a high Entry Opportunity score AND a low "Comp. Pressure" count (zero aggressive incumbents). On the Competitor Watch tab, a bank flagged "⚑ In Target Mkt" is the highest-priority watch item: it's an aggressive competitor sitting in a market we want, so it's simultaneously a defensive alert and an offensive signpost.

Scoring Components (3 factors, 100 points max)

        


          

            

              Low Marginal Rate (county IB cost of funds)
              0-45 pts
            

            

              What it measures: The county's marginal rate to win — the median interest-bearing cost of funds (annualized EINTEXP ÷ interest-bearing deposits) across every bank with a branch in the county. We also surface the maximum IB COF (the single most aggressive incumbent).

              How it works: Lower median rate vs. the statewide county median scores higher (1.0 well below median, 0.5 at median, → 0 at 2× median).

              Why it matters: Where incumbents pay a low marginal rate, Ranger can win rate-sensitive deposits cheaply. Where the rate is already bid up, entry means an expensive rate war. This is a real, complete FDIC signal — it covers every county, including those with no scraped advertised rates.
            

          


          

            

              Low Competition (few aggressive incumbents)
              0-35 pts
            

            

              What it measures: How many banks operating in the county land in the Competitor Watch Hot/Warm bands (aggressive marginal-rate bidders).

              How it works: Zero aggressive incumbents scores full; the score tapers as more aggressive competitors operate in the county.

              Why it matters: A county already contested by aggressive rate-payers is a fight to enter; an uncontested county is a clean green-light.
            

          


          

            

              Market Size (Bank Count, U-curve)
              0-20 pts
            

            

              What it measures: Distinct banks with a branch in the county.

              How it works: U-curve — 3–10 banks is the sweet spot (real market depth without saturation); very few banks (thin market) or a very crowded metro both score lower.

              Why it matters: Enough deposit demand to be worth entering, without the crowding that turns entry into a brawl.
            

          

        


        

          On marketed rates from national competitors. National banks render rates client-side from backend APIs (Capital One's static HTML even serves NaN), so a plain static scrape returns nothing. A ToS-cleared headless-browser pass (Playwright/Chromium) with per-bank parsers now renders them, and three of the four biggest are live in the data with full provenance and an explicit rate tier:
          Capital One — full 360 CD ladder (9 terms, standard, $0 min; e.g. 12-mo 3.90%);
          Chase — featured relationship CDs (2-mo 1.50%, 4-mo 3.30%, 11-mo 2.75%; require a linked Chase checking account, $1,000 min) — captured from Chase's labeled rate table and flagged RELATIONSHIP so they're never read as unconditional;
          Wells Fargo — 4-mo Special Fixed Rate CD 3.49% ($5,000 min). Bank of America's rate DOM didn't yield a clean, stable capture and is excluded pending a dedicated parser (no guessing). Each rate carries its conditions on hover in the Rate Scanner; advertised-rate coverage is now 99 banks.
          On the in-state statewide competitors: we swept all 255 uncovered TX-HQ banks for advertised rates; only a handful publish parseable APYs online — the large regionals (Prosperity, Frost, Woodforest, First Financial) post no numeric rates on the public web (by-branch / PDF / call-only). Where a TX bank does publish a statewide rate it's tagged state scope and attributed to every county in that bank's branch footprint. For the rest, the complete in-county competitive signal is the FDIC interest-bearing cost of funds — the realized marginal rate each of the 457 banks actually pays, already attributed to all of its footprint counties.
          Separately, the national-average benchmarks (the "vs national" comparisons) are sourced from the FDIC's official weekly National Rates & Rate Caps (e.g. 12-mo CD national avg 1.55%, savings 0.38%, pulled 2026-06-09).
          The county map's rate signal remains the FDIC interest-bearing cost of funds — real, sourced, and complete for all 457 TX-branch banks — with these advertised rates layered in (county-specific rates attributed only to their county; national rates across the bank's footprint).
